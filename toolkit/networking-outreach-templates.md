# Networking Outreach Templates

Ready-to-use templates for DMs, emails, and introductions. Customise each one to the specific person you're reaching out to — personalisation is what separates a great message from spam.

---

## How to Use These Templates

1. **Never copy-paste without editing.** Each template is a starting framework. Personalise it with specific details about the person and their work.
2. **Keep it short.** Busy people appreciate brevity. Get to the point quickly.
3. **Lead with value.** Give before you ask. Compliment, share, recommend — then make your request.
4. **Make the ask small.** The easier it is to say yes, the more likely they will.
5. **Follow up once.** If you don't hear back in 7-10 days, send one follow-up. After that, move on.

## Template 1: The Warm Connection Request (LinkedIn)

**Use when:** You want to connect with someone whose work you admire but haven't interacted with yet.

> Hi [Name],
>
> I came across your [post/article/talk] on [specific topic] and it really resonated — especially your point about [specific insight].
>
> I'm a [your role] working on [what you do], and I'd love to be connected here. Your work on [their area] is exactly the kind of thinking I want more of in my feed.
>
> Looking forward to learning from your content.
>
> [Your name]

---

## Template 2: The Thoughtful DM (LinkedIn or Twitter/X)

**Use when:** You've been engaging with someone's content and want to start a conversation.

> Hi [Name],
>
> I've been following your work on [topic] for a while now, and I wanted to reach out directly to say thank you. Your post about [specific post] genuinely changed how I think about [subject].
>
> I'm currently working on [your relevant project/focus], and your perspective on [specific area] has been incredibly helpful.
>
> No ask here — just wanted to let you know your work is making an impact.
>
> [Your name]

---

## Template 3: The "Pick Your Brain" Request (Done Right)

**Use when:** You want to ask someone a specific question. Note: don't ask for a "coffee chat" or to "pick their brain" without being specific about what you want to know.

> Hi [Name],
>
> I've been following your journey in [their field/area] and really admire how you've [specific achievement or approach].
>
> I'm working on [your specific challenge] and I have one specific question I'd love your perspective on:
>
> [Your specific, well-thought-out question]
>
> I know your time is valuable, so even a quick reply would mean a lot. And if you've written about this anywhere, happy to read that instead.
>
> Thanks so much,
> [Your name]

## Template 4: The Collaboration Pitch

**Use when:** You want to propose a collaboration — joint content, interview, event, etc.

> Hi [Name],
>
> I'm [your name], a [your role] focused on [your area]. I've been a fan of your work on [specific topic] — your [specific content piece] was especially insightful.
>
> I'm reaching out because I think there's a great opportunity for us to collaborate on [specific idea]. Here's what I had in mind:
>
> **The idea:** [2-3 sentences describing the collaboration]
> **Why it works:** [1-2 sentences on why it benefits both of you]
> **What I need from you:** [Be specific and make it easy]
>
> I've already [any prep work you've done], so this would be [easy/low-lift] on your end.
>
> Would you be open to exploring this? Happy to jump on a quick call or chat more here.
>
> [Your name]

## Template 5: The Cold Email

**Use when:** Reaching out to someone you don't have an existing connection with, typically via email.

**Subject line:** [Specific and intriguing — not "Quick question" or "Reaching out"]

Examples:
- "Your [talk/article] on [topic] — a question from a fellow [role]"
- "Loved your take on [specific topic] — [your angle]"
- "[Mutual connection] suggested I reach out"

> Hi [Name],
>
> My name is [your name] — I'm a [your role] based in [location], working on [what you do in one sentence].
>
> I came across your [specific work — article, talk, project, company] and wanted to reach out because [specific reason this is relevant to both of you].
>
> [One paragraph of value: a compliment, a shared interest, a resource, or a relevant insight]
>
> [Your ask — keep it small and specific]:
> - "Would you be open to a 15-minute call to discuss [topic]?"
> - "I'd love to hear your thoughts on [specific question]"
> - "Would you be interested in [specific collaboration idea]?"
>
> Totally understand if the timing doesn't work — just grateful for the work you put out there.
>
> Best,
> [Your name]
> [Your LinkedIn/website — one link only]

## Template 6: The Follow-Up After Meeting

**Use when:** You met someone at an event, conference, or virtual meetup and want to stay connected.

> Hi [Name],
>
> It was great meeting you at [event name] — I really enjoyed our conversation about [specific topic you discussed].
>
> Your point about [something specific they said] stuck with me, and I've been thinking about how it applies to [your work or context].
>
> I'd love to stay connected and continue the conversation. [Optional: here's that resource/article/link I mentioned.]
>
> Looking forward to keeping in touch.
>
> [Your name]

## Template 7: The Introduction Request

**Use when:** You want to ask a mutual connection to introduce you to someone.

> Hi [Mutual connection's name],
>
> Hope you're doing well! I wanted to ask if you'd be comfortable introducing me to [person's name]. I've been following their work on [topic] and think there could be a great opportunity to [reason — collaborate, learn, discuss something specific].
>
> Here's a short blurb you can forward if that's easier:
>
> *"Hi [person's name], I wanted to introduce you to [your name]. [He/She/They] is a [your role] who works on [what you do]. [He/She/They] has been doing interesting work on [specific area] and I thought you two would have a great conversation about [topic]. I'll let you both take it from here!"*
>
> No pressure at all — and thanks for even considering it.
>
> [Your name]

## Template 8: The Follow-Up (When You Don't Hear Back)

**Use when:** You sent a message 7-10 days ago and haven't received a reply.

> Hi [Name],
>
> Just wanted to bump this to the top of your inbox in case it got buried. I know things get busy!
>
> [One sentence summarising your original ask]
>
> Totally understand if the timing isn't right — no pressure at all.
>
> [Your name]

**Important:** Send only one follow-up. If you still don't hear back, move on gracefully. They may respond later, or not — and that's okay.

---

## General Tips

- **Personalise everything.** Generic messages get ignored. Specific messages get responses.
- **Keep it under 150 words.** For DMs and initial outreach, shorter is better.
- **Don't apologise for reaching out.** You're not bothering anyone by being professional and thoughtful.
- **Give before you ask.** Share their work, comment on their posts, offer a resource. Build goodwill first.
- **Be patient.** Not everyone responds immediately. Give it time.
- **Accept rejection gracefully.** A "no" (or silence) isn't personal. Move on to the next connection.

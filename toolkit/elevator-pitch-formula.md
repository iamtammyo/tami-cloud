# Elevator Pitch Formula

Your elevator pitch is the spoken version of your personal brand. It's what you say when someone asks, "So, what do you do?" — and you have 30-60 seconds to make an impression.

This worksheet will help you craft a pitch that's clear, memorable, and natural.

---

## The Problem With Most Pitches

Most people answer "What do you do?" with their job title:

> "I'm a marketing manager at [Company]."

This tells people nothing about your value, your expertise, or why they should care. It's forgettable — and it ends the conversation instead of starting one.

---

## The Formula

A strong elevator pitch has three parts:

### Part 1: The Problem You Solve

Start with the problem or challenge your audience faces. This immediately makes the conversation relevant to the listener.

> "You know how [problem your audience experiences]?"

### Part 2: What You Do About It

Explain your role and how you address that problem.

> "I [what you do] to help [who you help] [achieve what outcome]."

### Part 3: The Hook

End with something that invites further conversation — a result, a surprising fact, or a question.

> "[Proof point, interesting detail, or conversation starter]"

---

## Examples

### Example 1: Content Strategist

> "You know how so many talented professionals are doing incredible work but no one outside their company knows about it? I help early-career professionals build personal brands that make them visible to global opportunities. Last year, three of my clients landed international roles through content they created using my frameworks."

### Example 2: Product Designer

> "A lot of startups build products that are technically brilliant but confusing to use. I design user experiences for fintech companies that make complex financial tools feel simple and intuitive. The last product I redesigned saw a 40% drop in support tickets within a month."

### Example 3: Freelance Writer

> "Most founders know they need content to grow, but they don't have time to write it — and generic content doesn't convert. I write strategic content for SaaS startups that turns readers into customers. One of my recent articles drove more signups than the client's entire paid ad campaign."

### Example 4: Career Coach

> "You know how professionals often feel stuck — like they're working hard but not moving forward? I coach ambitious mid-career professionals through transitions, helping them figure out what's next and how to get there. I've helped over 200 people land roles they didn't think were possible."

## The Gut Check

Read your pitch out loud. Then ask:

- [ ] Can someone with no context understand what I do?
- [ ] Does it sound like a conversation, not a presentation?
- [ ] Does it communicate value, not just activities?
- [ ] Would someone respond with a follow-up question? (That's the goal.)
- [ ] Does it feel natural when I say it?

---

## Adapting Your Pitch

You'll need slightly different versions for different contexts:

| Context | Length | Focus |
|---------|--------|-------|
| Networking event | 30 seconds | Problem + solution + hook |
| Conference introduction | 15 seconds | One-line positioning statement |
| Job interview | 60 seconds | Full pitch with more context on your background |
| Online bio | 1-2 sentences | Written version of your positioning |
| Casual conversation | 10 seconds | The simplest version: "I help [who] [do what]" |

### The One-Liner Version

Distill your pitch to a single sentence:

> "I help [who] [achieve what]."

Examples:
- "I help professionals build brands that open doors globally."
- "I design fintech products that people actually enjoy using."
- "I write content that turns readers into customers."

---

## Practice Makes Natural

Your pitch shouldn't sound rehearsed. It should sound like you're explaining what you do to a curious friend. Here's how to get there:

1. **Write it out** — Use the formula above
2. **Say it out loud** — Record yourself if it helps
3. **Test it on friends** — Ask: "Did that make sense? Would you want to know more?"
4. **Refine based on reactions** — Keep what lands, cut what confuses
5. **Practice until it's effortless** — You should be able to deliver it without thinking

---

## Remember

Your elevator pitch isn't a monologue — it's a conversation starter. The goal isn't to say everything about yourself. It's to say enough that the other person wants to learn more.

# LinkedIn Profile Optimization Guide

A step-by-step guide to overhauling your LinkedIn profile so it works as a 24/7 brand asset — not just a digital CV.

---

## Before You Start

Open your LinkedIn profile in another tab. You'll work through each section below, making updates as you go.

**Time needed:** 45-60 minutes for a full overhaul.

---

## Section 1: Profile Photo

**Goal:** Look professional, approachable, and recognisable.

### Checklist

- [ ] Photo is recent (within the last 1-2 years)
- [ ] Your face takes up 60-70% of the frame
- [ ] Good lighting (natural light is best)
- [ ] Clean, uncluttered background
- [ ] Genuine, approachable expression
- [ ] High resolution (not blurry or pixelated)
- [ ] It's just you — no group photos, no pets, no logos

**Quick fix:** If you don't have a professional photo, use a smartphone with good natural light. Stand near a window, use portrait mode, and wear what you'd wear to a professional event in your industry.

---

## Section 2: Banner Image

**Goal:** Use this free branding space to communicate your positioning at a glance.

### Options

| Approach | Best For | How To |
|----------|----------|--------|
| Text-based banner | Most professionals | Use Canva — add your positioning statement or key phrase against a branded background |
| Achievement banner | Speakers, authors, award winners | Feature a key accomplishment with a supporting visual |
| Visual brand banner | Designers, creatives | Showcase your aesthetic or portfolio highlights |

### Checklist

- [ ] Banner is not the default LinkedIn graphic
- [ ] Banner communicates something about your brand (not just a nice photo)
- [ ] Text is readable on both desktop and mobile
- [ ] Banner dimensions are 1584 x 396 pixels

---

## Section 3: Headline

**Goal:** Communicate what you do and who you help in under 220 characters.

### Formula

> [What you do] | Helping [who] [achieve what]

### Examples

| Instead of this... | Try this... |
|--------------------|-------------|
| "Marketing Manager at Company X" | "Marketing Manager | Helping B2B startups build brands that resonate globally" |
| "Software Engineer" | "Software Engineer | Building fintech products that make financial services accessible" |
| "Freelance Writer" | "Freelance Writer | Helping founders and startups tell stories that attract customers" |

### Checklist

- [ ] Headline goes beyond your job title
- [ ] It mentions who you help or what outcome you create
- [ ] It includes relevant keywords for searchability
- [ ] It would make sense to someone outside your company

---

## Section 4: About Section

**Goal:** Tell your brand story in 3-4 paragraphs that hook, connect, and convert.

### Structure

**Paragraph 1 — The Hook**
Open with a bold statement, question, or observation that captures attention.

*Example: "Most talented professionals are invisible — not because they lack skill, but because no one taught them how to be seen."*

**Paragraph 2 — The Problem**
Describe the challenge your audience faces. This shows you understand their world.

*Example: "If you're an ambitious professional doing great work but not getting the recognition, opportunities, or visibility you deserve — you're not alone. And it's not about working harder."*

**Paragraph 3 — Your Solution**
Explain what you do and how you help. This is your positioning statement in narrative form.

*Example: "I help early-career professionals build personal brands that attract global opportunities. Through content strategy, positioning, and authentic storytelling, I teach people how to become known for what they're great at."*

**Paragraph 4 — Proof + CTA**
Mention key achievements or results, then tell people what to do next.

*Example: "I've helped X professionals across Y countries build their brands online. If you're ready to invest in your visibility, connect with me or send me a DM."*

### Checklist

- [ ] Opens with a hook, not "I am a..."
- [ ] Speaks to your audience's challenges
- [ ] Clearly communicates what you do and for whom
- [ ] Includes at least one proof point
- [ ] Ends with a clear call to action

---

## Section 5: Featured

**Goal:** Showcase your best work in a curated highlight reel.

### What to Feature (pick 3-5)

- [ ] Your best-performing LinkedIn post
- [ ] A published article, blog post, or newsletter
- [ ] A talk, presentation, or podcast appearance
- [ ] A case study or portfolio piece
- [ ] Your website or landing page

### Checklist

- [ ] Featured items are recent and relevant
- [ ] They reinforce your positioning (not random achievements)
- [ ] Thumbnails look clean and professional
- [ ] Links work and lead to the right destinations

---

## Section 6: Experience

**Goal:** Show impact and value, not just responsibilities.

### Formula for Each Role

> [What you achieved] + [How you did it] + [The result or impact]

### Before & After

| Before | After |
|--------|-------|
| "Managed social media accounts" | "Grew social media following by 300% in 6 months through a data-driven content strategy, generating 50+ qualified leads per month" |
| "Wrote blog posts" | "Created a content programme that drove 40% of the company's organic traffic, publishing 3 long-form articles per week" |
| "Led a team of designers" | "Led a 5-person design team that shipped 12 product features, reducing user onboarding time by 35%" |

### Checklist

- [ ] Each role leads with impact, not tasks
- [ ] Includes specific numbers where possible
- [ ] Uses active, results-oriented language
- [ ] Most recent role is the most detailed

---

## Section 7: Skills & Endorsements

**Goal:** Reinforce your positioning through strategic keyword selection.

### Checklist

- [ ] Your top 3 pinned skills align with your positioning
- [ ] Skills include relevant industry keywords (for search)
- [ ] You've removed skills that dilute your brand
- [ ] You've endorsed others strategically (they often return the favour)

---

## Section 8: Recommendations

**Goal:** Build social proof through third-party endorsements.

### How to Get Recommendations

1. Make a list of 5-10 people who've seen your best work
2. Send a specific request: "Would you be willing to write a short recommendation about [specific project/skill]? It would really help strengthen my profile."
3. Offer to write one for them first — reciprocity works

### Checklist

- [ ] You have at least 3 recommendations
- [ ] Recommendations mention specific skills or outcomes
- [ ] They come from a mix of managers, peers, and clients
- [ ] The most recent one is from the past 12 months

---

## Final Review

Open your profile and review it as if you're seeing it for the first time. Ask:

- [ ] Does this profile clearly communicate what I do and who I help?
- [ ] Would my target audience feel like this person "gets" them?
- [ ] Is there a clear reason for someone to connect with me or reach out?
- [ ] Does every section reinforce the same brand message?

If yes — you're in great shape. If not — revisit the sections that feel off.

---

## Maintenance Schedule

| Task | Frequency |
|------|-----------|
| Update headline and about section | Every 3-6 months |
| Refresh featured section | Monthly |
| Add new experience or achievements | As they happen |
| Request new recommendations | Quarterly |
| Review and update skills | Every 6 months |
| Update profile photo | Annually |

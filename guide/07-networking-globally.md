# Chapter 7: Networking Globally

Your personal brand doesn't exist in a vacuum. It grows through relationships — with peers, mentors, collaborators, clients, and community members.

This chapter is about building those relationships intentionally, especially across borders. Because in a global, digital-first world, your network isn't limited by geography. It's limited by your willingness to reach out.

---

## Why Global Networking Matters

If you've followed the chapters so far, you have:
- A clear brand foundation (Chapter 2)
- A defined audience (Chapter 3)
- Sharp positioning (Chapter 4)
- An optimised online presence (Chapter 5)
- A content strategy (Chapter 6)

Networking is what turns all of that from a solo effort into a connected one. Here's why it matters for global career growth specifically:

**Opportunities cross borders.** The person who refers you for a remote role might be in Berlin. The collaborator who invites you to speak at a conference might be in Toronto. The client who finds your work valuable might be in Singapore. Global networking expands the surface area for opportunity.

**Relationships accelerate credibility.** When respected people in your field engage with your content, share your work, or endorse your skills, their credibility transfers to you. This is especially powerful when building recognition in a new market.

**Community beats competition.** The professionals who build the strongest brands aren't isolated — they're embedded in communities. They lift others up, and in return, they're lifted too.

## The Networking Mindset

Before we talk tactics, let's address the mindset that makes networking work:

### Networking is not transactional

The goal isn't to collect connections or "use" people. It's to build genuine relationships where value flows both ways. When you approach networking as service — how can I help, support, or add value to this person? — the outcomes follow naturally.

### Networking is not just for extroverts

You don't need to be the loudest person in the room (or on the platform). Some of the most effective networkers are thoughtful introverts who build deep, one-on-one relationships. Quality over quantity — always.

### Networking is a long game

The connections you build today may not "pay off" for months or years. That's fine. Relationships are investments, not transactions. Show up consistently, and the compounding effect will take care of the rest.

## Online Networking: The Three Layers

### Layer 1: Engage With Content

The easiest and most underrated way to network online is through engagement. Leave thoughtful comments on posts from people you admire or want to connect with.

**What makes a comment valuable:**
- It adds a new perspective or insight to the conversation
- It shares a relevant personal experience
- It asks a thoughtful follow-up question
- It's specific — not just "Great post!" or a string of emojis

**The impact:** When you consistently leave thoughtful comments on someone's content, they notice. You become familiar. And when you eventually reach out via DM or send a connection request, you're not a stranger — you're someone who's been adding value in their world.

### Layer 2: Direct Outreach

Once you've built some familiarity through engagement, you can reach out directly. This works via DM on LinkedIn, Twitter/X, or email.

**The anatomy of a good outreach message:**

1. **Personalisation** — Reference something specific about their work
2. **Value** — Offer something useful (a resource, an introduction, a compliment that shows you've paid attention)
3. **Low-pressure ask** — If you're requesting something, make it small and easy to say yes to

**Examples:**

> "Hi [Name], I've been following your work on [specific topic] and your post about [specific post] really changed how I think about [subject]. I'm working on something similar in [your context] and would love to hear your perspective on [specific question]. No pressure at all — just grateful for the insight you share publicly."

> "Hi [Name], I noticed we're both working in [shared space]. Your article on [topic] was one of the best things I've read on the subject. I recently published something related — [link] — and I'd love to know what you think."

### Layer 3: Collaboration

The deepest level of networking is building things together. This could mean:

- Co-creating content (joint articles, interview series, podcast appearances)
- Collaborating on projects (events, workshops, community initiatives)
- Referring opportunities to each other
- Building a peer group or mastermind

Collaboration creates shared audiences, mutual credibility, and relationships that go beyond "networking" into genuine partnership.

## Offline Networking in a Global Context

In-person events haven't disappeared — they've become more valuable precisely because they're less common.

### Conferences and Events

- **Before the event:** Research speakers and attendees. Engage with their content ahead of time so you're not a stranger.
- **At the event:** Focus on having 3-5 meaningful conversations rather than collecting 50 business cards.
- **After the event:** Follow up within 48 hours. Reference something specific from your conversation.

### Local Meetups and Communities

Even if your brand is global, local communities are powerful. Industry meetups, co-working spaces, and professional groups in your city can become launching pads for global connections.

### Virtual Events

Webinars, Twitter/X Spaces, LinkedIn Live events, and online conferences are accessible from anywhere. Participate actively — ask questions, share insights in the chat, and follow up with speakers and participants afterward.

## Building a Network That Works for You

Not all connections are equal. Be intentional about the types of relationships you build:

| Type | Who They Are | Why They Matter |
|------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Peers** | People at your level in your field | Mutual support, shared growth, collaboration |
| **Mentors** | People 5-10 years ahead of you | Guidance, perspective, door-opening |
| **Champions** | People who actively promote your work | Amplification, referrals, endorsements |
| **Community** | People in your niche or audience | Engagement, feedback, belonging |
| **Cross-industry** | People in adjacent fields | Fresh perspectives, unexpected opportunities |

You don't need hundreds of each. A few strong relationships in each category will transform your career.

## Common Networking Mistakes

**Leading with asks.** Don't DM someone you've never interacted with and ask them to be your mentor, review your work, or refer you for a job. Build the relationship first.

**Being generic.** "I'd love to connect!" means nothing. Be specific about why you're reaching out and what you appreciate about their work.

**Disappearing after getting what you want.** Networking isn't a one-time transaction. Stay in touch, share their work, celebrate their wins. Relationships require maintenance.

**Only networking when you need something.** Build relationships before you need them. The best time to network is when you have nothing to ask for.

**Ignoring people at your level.** Your peers today are tomorrow's leaders, hiring managers, and collaborators. Don't only network "up."

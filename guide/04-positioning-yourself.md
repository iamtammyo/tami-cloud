# Chapter 4: Positioning Yourself

You've identified your values, your strengths, your story, and your audience. Now it's time to bring it all together into something strategic: your positioning.

Positioning is the art of occupying a distinct space in your audience's mind. It's the answer to: "When someone thinks of [your topic], do they think of you?"

If your brand foundation is who you are, positioning is how you show up relative to everyone else.

---

## What Positioning Really Means

Positioning isn't about being better than everyone else in your field. It's about being *different* in a way that matters to the people you serve.

Think about it this way:

- There are thousands of marketing professionals online. But there's likely only one who combines your specific experience, perspective, and audience focus.
- There are hundreds of product designers sharing their work. But your particular approach — shaped by your context, your culture, your career path — is yours alone.

Positioning takes what's unique about you and makes it *strategic*. It turns your distinctiveness into a reason for the right people to pay attention.

---

## The Three Layers of Positioning

Strong positioning has three layers:

### Layer 1: Topic Authority

**What subject or area will you be known for?**

This should be specific enough to be ownable, but broad enough to sustain ongoing content and conversation.

Examples:
- Too broad: "Marketing" — you'll never own this
- Too narrow: "Email subject line A/B testing for fintech newsletters" — hard to sustain
- Just right: "Content strategy for B2B startups in emerging markets" — specific, credible, sustainable

### Layer 2: Point of View

**What do you believe about your topic that not everyone agrees with?**

A strong point of view is what separates thought leaders from content repeaters. It's not about being controversial for its own sake — it's about having a genuine perspective informed by your experience.

Examples:
- "I believe personal branding is more important than your CV in the modern job market."
- "I believe African tech talent doesn't have a skills gap — it has a visibility gap."
- "I believe most content strategies fail because they prioritise volume over depth."

Your point of view gives people a reason to follow you. It makes your content feel like it comes from somewhere — not like it was generated from a template.

### Layer 3: Audience Specificity

**Who exactly are you positioning yourself for?**

You did this work in Chapter 3. Now connect it to your topic and point of view.

The combination of these three layers is your positioning:

> I talk about [TOPIC] for [AUDIENCE] because I believe [POINT OF VIEW].

---

## Crafting Your Positioning Statement

Your positioning statement is an internal tool — a north star that keeps your brand focused. It doesn't need to be catchy. It needs to be clear.

### The Formula

> I help [specific audience] [achieve specific outcome] by [your unique approach or perspective].

### Examples

> "I help early-career professionals in Africa build personal brands that attract global opportunities — by showing them how to turn their experience into content that builds authority."

> "I help remote teams communicate more effectively across cultures — by applying principles from linguistics and behavioural psychology to everyday workplace interactions."

> "I help freelance designers win international clients — by teaching them how to present their work as strategic business solutions, not just pretty deliverables."

### Exercise: Write Your Positioning Statement

Using the formula above, draft your statement. Don't overthink it. You'll refine it over time. What matters now is having a working version that captures:

1. **Who** you serve
2. **What** outcome you help them achieve
3. **How** you do it differently

---

## Testing Your Positioning

Good positioning passes three tests:

### The Clarity Test
> Can someone who's never met you understand what you do and who you do it for within 10 seconds of reading your positioning statement?

If they can't, simplify.

### The Specificity Test
> Could someone else in your field use the exact same positioning statement?

If yes, you're not specific enough. Add more of your unique angle.

### The Resonance Test
> When your ideal audience member reads your positioning, do they think "this person gets me"?

If not, you might be too focused on what *you* do and not enough on what *they* need.

---

## Positioning Pitfalls

**Being too vague.** "I help people succeed" tells no one anything. Get ruthlessly specific about who, what, and how.

**Copying someone else's positioning.** You can be inspired by others, but your positioning needs to come from your own intersection of experience, expertise, and perspective. If it sounds like someone else, it won't feel authentic — and people can tell.

**Over-positioning too early.** Your positioning is a working hypothesis, not a permanent contract. Start with your best guess, put it out there, and refine it based on what resonates. Perfectionism here is a trap.

**Ignoring the competition.** You don't need to obsess over competitors, but you should be aware of who else occupies your space. Your positioning should differentiate you — and that's hard to do if you don't know what others are saying.

## Your Positioning in Practice

Once you have your positioning statement, it should influence everything:

| Where It Shows Up | How It Shapes Your Decisions |
|-------------------|------------------------------|
| LinkedIn headline | Your headline should reflect your positioning, not just your job title |
| Content topics | Every piece of content should reinforce what you want to be known for |
| Speaking & events | Say yes to opportunities that align with your positioning, no to those that dilute it |
| Networking | Introduce yourself in a way that communicates your positioning in one sentence |
| Projects & collaborations | Partner with people and brands that reinforce, not confuse, your brand |

---

## Chapter Summary

- Positioning is how you occupy a distinct space in your audience's mind.
- Strong positioning combines topic authority, a clear point of view, and audience specificity.
- Your positioning statement is: "I help [audience] [achieve outcome] by [unique approach]."
- Test your positioning for clarity, specificity, and resonance.
- Your positioning should guide every brand decision — from your LinkedIn headline to the collaborations you accept.

---

## Action Steps

1. Define your topic authority: what specific subject will you be known for?
2. Articulate your point of view: what do you believe that sets you apart?
3. Write your positioning statement using the formula.
4. Run your statement through the three tests (clarity, specificity, resonance).
5. Update your LinkedIn headline to reflect your positioning.

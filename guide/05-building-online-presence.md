# Chapter 5: Building Your Online Presence

Your online presence is the digital handshake that happens before anyone ever talks to you. It's where first impressions are formed, credibility is assessed, and opportunities are won or lost — often without you knowing.

This chapter is about making sure that when someone finds you online, what they see accurately reflects the brand you've built in the previous chapters.

---

## The Digital First Impression

Here's the reality of how most professional relationships start in a global context:

1. Someone hears your name or sees your content
2. They Google you or find your LinkedIn
3. In 30 seconds or less, they decide whether to engage further

That 30-second window is your digital first impression. And it's happening constantly — with recruiters, potential clients, collaborators, conference organisers, and peers.

The question isn't whether people are looking you up. They are. The question is: what do they find?

---

## Choosing Your Primary Platform

You don't need to be everywhere. You need to be *excellent* somewhere.

For most professionals building a global brand, the platform hierarchy looks like this:

### Tier 1: Your Home Base (pick one)

| Platform | Best For |
|----------|----------|
| **LinkedIn** | Corporate professionals, B2B, career growth, thought leadership |
| **Twitter/X** | Tech, media, startup ecosystems, real-time conversation |
| **Personal website/blog** | Long-form authority building, creative professionals, portfolio |

This is where you invest the most time and energy. It's where your best content lives, where your profile is most optimised, and where your audience can find you consistently.

### Tier 2: Supporting Platforms (pick one or two)

These amplify your home base. You might repurpose content here, engage in communities, or maintain a polished profile.

Examples: Medium, Substack, YouTube, Instagram (for certain industries), industry-specific platforms.

### Tier 3: Profile-only

Platforms where you have an updated profile but don't actively create content. Keep them current but don't stress about them.

**The rule:** Go deep on one platform before spreading thin across many. Depth beats breadth.

## Optimising Your LinkedIn Profile

For the majority of professionals reading this guide, LinkedIn is your highest-leverage platform. Let's optimise it section by section.

### Your Profile Photo

- Professional, recent, and high quality
- Your face should take up about 60-70% of the frame
- Good lighting, clean background, genuine expression
- This is not the place for group photos, logos, or overly casual images

### Your Banner Image

This is free branding real estate that most people waste. Use it to:
- State what you do and who you help
- Showcase your expertise or a key achievement
- Feature your brand colours or a relevant visual

You can create a simple banner in Canva in under 10 minutes.

### Your Headline

Your headline is the most important text on your profile. It appears in search results, comments, connection requests — everywhere.

**Don't just list your job title.** Instead, use the formula:

> [What you do] | Helping [who] [achieve what]

Examples:
- "Content Strategist | Helping professionals build brands that attract global opportunities"
- "Product Designer | Creating user experiences for fintech startups across Africa"
- "Marketing Consultant | Helping B2B startups communicate their value to international markets"

### Your About Section

This is your brand story in 3-4 paragraphs. Structure it like this:

1. **Hook** — Open with a statement or question that captures attention
2. **Problem** — Describe the challenge your audience faces (this shows you understand them)
3. **Your approach** — Explain what you do and how you help (this is your positioning)
4. **Proof** — Mention key achievements, experiences, or results
5. **Call to action** — Tell people what to do next (connect, DM, visit your website)

### Your Experience Section

Don't just list responsibilities. For each role:
- Lead with the impact you made, not the tasks you performed
- Use specific numbers and results where possible
- Frame your experience in terms of the value you delivered

### Your Featured Section

Pin your best content here:
- A viral or high-performing post
- A key article or blog piece
- A presentation or talk
- Your portfolio or website link

This section is your highlight reel. Curate it intentionally.

## Making Your Profiles Consistent

Wherever you have an online presence, ensure consistency in:

- **Name format** — Use the same name across platforms
- **Photo** — Use the same (or similar) professional photo
- **Bio/headline** — Your core message should be recognisable across platforms
- **Links** — Cross-link your profiles so people can find you everywhere

Consistency builds recognition. When someone sees you on LinkedIn, then on Twitter, then on a podcast, they should immediately know it's the same person with the same message.

---

## The Google Test

Search your own name. What comes up?

If the results don't reflect your brand, here are ways to improve:

1. **Optimise your LinkedIn** — It typically ranks first for name searches
2. **Publish content** — Articles, blog posts, and guest pieces create more searchable results
3. **Claim your name** — Register your name as a domain, even if you don't build a full website yet
4. **Be active in indexed spaces** — Platforms like Medium, Substack, and YouTube rank well in search

Your goal: when someone Googles you, the first page of results tells a coherent story about who you are and what you're known for.

## Chapter Summary

- Your online presence is your digital first impression — and it's happening whether you manage it or not.
- Choose one primary platform and go deep before going wide.
- Optimise your LinkedIn profile section by section: photo, banner, headline, about, experience, and featured content.
- Maintain consistency across all platforms in name, photo, messaging, and links.
- Regularly Google yourself and ensure the results reflect your brand.
